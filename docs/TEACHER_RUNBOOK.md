# Teacher and Coach Runbook

## Purpose

EL Lesson Alignment Tool helps content teachers turn one existing lesson into a CA ELD-aligned lesson plus a readable visual packet. The packet is generated from the checked alignment output, not from raw uploaded files.

## Teacher Workflow: 10 Minutes

1. Remove student names, IDs, scores, IEP/504 details, health details, rosters, and family contact information from the lesson.
2. Paste the lesson or upload a `.txt`, `.md`, `.csv`, `.docx`, or selectable-text `.pdf`.
3. Choose grade band and content area.
4. Keep ELPAC focus at Levels 1 and 3 unless the beta later adds more controls.
5. Add a lesson goal or content expectation when it helps the model understand the task.
6. Click **Align Lesson**.
7. Review the **Visual Packet** first for a quick planning view.
8. Open **Aligned Lesson** to copy the source-of-truth lesson text.
9. Open **Rationale** to check CA ELD Part I, Part II, and PLD alignment.
10. Copy the Run ID when sending feedback.

## How To Review The Visual Packet

Check that the packet answers these questions:

- What content learning is the lesson aiming for?
- What must students say, do, or write as evidence of learning?
- What language function and language resources are needed?
- Are CA ELD Part I, Part II, and PLD shown together?
- Do ELPAC 1 and ELPAC 3 supports preserve the same cognitive expectation?
- Are sentence frames, success criteria, and teacher moves usable tomorrow?

The visual packet is a planning aid. The aligned lesson text remains the copy/edit source of truth.

## Coach Protocol: 30 Minutes

1. Read the original lesson task and name the content expectation.
2. Review the Language Demand section with the teacher.
3. Ask whether student evidence is observable in speaking, writing, reading, or discussion.
4. Review the CA ELD rationale as a connected bundle: Part I purpose, Part II language resources, and PLD access point.
5. Compare ELPAC 1 and ELPAC 3 supports. Confirm they change access, not the learning target.
6. Choose the two or three additions the teacher will actually use.
7. Capture feedback with Run ID, grade/content, overall score, and suggested better language.

## District Calibration Protocol

1. Collect 10-20 packets across grade bands, content areas, and lesson formats.
2. Score each packet with the SME rubric.
3. Look for consistency in language-demand naming, ELPAC supports, standards rationale, and teacher usability.
4. Identify output patterns that should become prompt or schema improvements.
5. Do not use packets that were generated from inputs containing student-identifiable data.

## Feedback Format

```text
Run ID:
Grade/content:
Lesson format:
Overall usefulness, 1-4:

What worked:

What was inaccurate, generic, or too much:

Did the visual packet help within 2 minutes?

Did CA ELD Part I + Part II + PLD rationale make sense?

Would a content teacher use this tomorrow? Why or why not?
```

## Privacy And Safety

Do not paste student-identifiable information into the beta. If the app blocks the input, remove private details and try again. The visual packet endpoint should only receive the checked alignment response and compact packet content, not raw uploaded files.
